## Why the lab and not the bench

`next/artifacts/p280-shape-profile/` records what the synthetic bench is: a forest where
every descriptor holds exactly 521 entries, every name is exactly 97 bytes, and there is
not one object-type GUID or one `SELF` entry in it. It was generated by this codebase, so
it can only contain what the code already believed - `F-S44-2`.

The lab is a real Active Directory. It is far too small for size or timing claims and
misleading about reuse, but it has the thing the bench cannot have: **the shapes nobody
thought to generate.** Its profile is at `lab-profile.json`.

## And what the bench could not have told us

| `permission_entry`, by trustee | Rows | Share |
|---|---|---|
| **`S-1-5-10`, `PRINCIPAL SELF`** | **34,593** | **94.8%** |
| Every other trustee in the forest | 1,891 | 5.2% |

**Ninety-five per cent of the table `P-279` built to shrink is one SID.**

`SharedPermissionStorage.ContentKey` serialises the whole `PermissionEntry` including
`Trustee.Name`, and `ObjectPermissions` resolves `S-1-5-10` to `This object (Alice)`
against the object being read. So Alice's `SELF` entry and Bob's hash differently and never
share a row, and every object carrying one gets its own. That is exactly what keeps `P-279`
*correct* - nothing is misattributed - and it is why the table it built barely deduplicates
on a real directory.

**The bench reported none of this**, because it has no `SELF` entries at all: 24,098 rows
against 17,164,866 occurrences, a 712:1 collapse, and a clean success. The lab says the
success is 5% of a real forest's rows.

`P-285` would take `permission_entry` here from 36,484 to roughly 1,900 - the raw `SELF`
ACE shapes collapse to a handful once the resolved name is out of the identity. On the
production capture the same defect accounts for **20,454,613 occurrences**.

## What this baseline is for

- **`P-285`** - re-judge and expect `permission_entry` near 1,900. That is the check.
- **`P-283`** - the binding change should alter no count here at all. A count that moves is
  the finding.
- **`P-280`** - `permission_classification_entry` should fall from 209,252 toward the
  per-descriptor figure. **The lab cannot price the saving**: its descriptors are reused
  22.3 times on average against production's 2.46, so keying per descriptor looks nine
  times better here than it will on the owner's forest. Correctness here, size elsewhere.

## Measured after `P-285`, 2026-09-25

Same evidence, same seal, a fresh copy judged by the build that keeps `SELF` as `SELF`.

| | Before | After | |
|---|---|---|---|
| `permission_entry`, `S-1-5-10` | 34,593 | **38** | −99.9% |
| `permission_entry`, every other trustee | 1,891 | 1,891 | unchanged |
| **`permission_entry`, total** | **36,484** | **1,929** | **−94.7%** |
| **`permission_classification_entry`** | **209,252** | **209,252** | **unchanged** |
| Judgement on disk | 107,888,640 | **95,526,912** | −11.5% |
| Judgement against its evidence | 9.3x | **8.2x** | |
| Rules ran / findings | 17 / 27 | 17 / 27 | identical |
| Judging wall clock | 22.8 s | 23.2 s | no regression |

**34,593 rows became 38**, which is the number of distinct raw `SELF` access control
entries this forest actually holds. The prediction written above before the change was
*"roughly 1,900"*; the measurement is 1,929.

**The occurrence count did not move, and that is the check that mattered.**
`permission_classification_entry` holds 209,252 rows before and after. Nothing here
deletes a permission: each of those rows is the distinct fact *this object, at this
position, carries that entry*, and an audit tool that lost one would be reporting a
permission the directory holds and the product does not mention. What changed is how many
times the entry behind them is written down.

**Rules and findings are identical**, so no verdict moved as a side effect of changing how
a trustee is stored.

**The store fell 11.5% and not 94.7%**, because `permission_entry` was never the large
table - `permission_classification_entry` is, at 209,252 rows, and shrinking that is
`P-280`. What `P-285` removed is the reason `P-279`'s saving did not survive contact with a
real forest, and it removes it before `P-280` chooses a sharing unit against a table that
was 95% one SID.

On the production capture the same defect accounts for **20,454,613 occurrences**, measured
in `next/artifacts/p280-shape-profile/`.

## Measured after `P-283`, 2026-09-25

The check written above before the change was *"the binding change should alter no count
here at all. A count that moves is the finding."*

| | After `P-285` | After `P-283` |
|---|---|---|
| `permission_entry` | 1,929 | **1,929** |
| `permission_classification_entry` | 209,252 | **209,252** |

**Nothing moved.** The store grew by 8,192 bytes - one page - because no column was
added: the reading's digest was folded into the occurrence fingerprint that was already
there. A separate binding column would have cost 32 bytes on every occurrence, which is
10.6 GB across the production capture's 330,856,576 and the opposite of what `P-280` is
for.
